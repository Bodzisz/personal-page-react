# The Diary Of A CEO
### by Steven Bartlett

The author - Steven Bartlett, is a 28-year-old CEO of Social Chain, a global social media marketing agency.

Think of this book as your personal mentor sitting across from you at a coffee shop, sharing life lessons learned the hard way.
Bartlett has distilled years of experience, countless podcast interviews, and his own journey into these 33 fundamental principles that shape both business and personal success.

In the book, we can find 4 pillars of principles:
- **Me**
- **The Story**
- **The Philosophy**
- **The Team**

Think of these pillars as different rooms in a house – each one serves a specific purpose but together they create a complete living space.

I won't quote all the principles and describe them in detail, but I will focus on explaining the pillars, what the author means by them, and how they connect.

---

## Me
The first pillar is ***"Me"***. This section focuses on building your personal foundation, much like constructing the basement and groundwork of a house.

The author teaches us that business success starts with understanding and developing ourselves. I love to repeat that self-awareness is the first step to everything. Like Socrates said:

> ***"All I know is that I know nothing."***

He explores concepts like **self-awareness**, **handling our emotions**, and **building resilience**. Author shows many tricks based on psychology that can help us with communication and marketing.
For example, Steven explains that when facts are in your favor, you should not state them, but ask the question about them. It encourages the other person to act.

The chapter about habits was specially interesting. Bartlett explains how habits work, how to implement good ones and get rid of the bad ones based on the circle of habits.

This pillar was probably the most impactful for me. For example, the two laws describing how to master something really changed my perspective on learning new skills.
Author showed that it is the teacher that learns the most and that to master something we need to do it consequently and publicly.

***This pillar is like a crash course in personal development, offering practical advice and psychological insights that can help you grow both personally and professionally.***

---

## The Story
If the first pillar is about  ***"The Story"***, this section is like learning how to present your house and build a story about it.

Here, Bartlett delves into the practical aspects of building and marketing successful ventures. This pillar covers many aspects about marketing, sales, and business strategy, all based on human psychology and his own expierence.
That was the thing that I loved about this pillar - everything was explained in a simple way, based on psychology.

One particularly insightful law discusses what he calls "Psychological Rockets" – they may work better than the actual improvements in your product or service.

For example - Uber. It shows the location of the delivery to keep the customer engaged and make them feel like they are in control.

Some services extends the time of the operation on purpose to make the client think that there are ton of operations going on under the hood and that the result is precise and well-thought.

Of course, I cannot skip the law about the absurdity speaking louder than the practical functionality.
The example couldn't be better - it is a story about him build a huge, blue slide from floor to floor when he was just starting with his office.
That costed 13k pounds, but it brought so much attention that I think was actually an amazing investment.

***This pillar is like a masterclass in marketing and business tricks.***

---

## The Philosophy
This category is like learning how to create the electrical and plumbing systems that connect everything in your house.

In this pillar Steven shows the approach to doing almost everything in life.
Like in the other chapters everything is bade on psychology and his own expierence.

You will find universal techniques and tips to help you make better decisions and have a strategy.

The law that spoke to me in this pillar was about pressure. The author explains that pressure is a privilege and that we should embrace it.
It is a sign that we are doing something important and that we are on the right path.

Amongs other laws, author also reccomends to beat the competition in the number of failed attempts. You should fail fast.
This way you can learn from your mistakes fast and improve your approach.

***Again, golden set of rules about buisness and life strategy.***

---

## The Team
Think of this as learning how to make your house truly feel like a home – have the right people there.

Author explains how to build a right mentality in the firm and what it takes to be a great leader.

If you like football you will smile when Steven uses an anegdote about Sir Alex Ferguson and his approach to the team.

***This pillar is useful both for people who want to become a leader and for those who already manage a team.***

---

## Summary
What makes these categories particularly effective is how they interweave and support each other.
Just as a house needs all its systems working together to function properly, Bartlett shows how mastering these different areas creates a synergy that leads to both success and fulfillment.

Book is really well-written. One of the laws is about paying attention to every little detail and by
reading this book you can really know that author uses the laws he describes.

I can definetely reccomend the book. If you are into marketing, psychology, entrepreneurship, or personal development, you will find it valuable. It's a great read for anyone looking to build a successful business and a meaningful life.
